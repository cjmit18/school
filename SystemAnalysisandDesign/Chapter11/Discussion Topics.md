2. What are the most significant differences among structured, O-O, and agile methods? What do they have in common?



Diligent coding practices and keeping to a specific coding format allows programmers to avoid mistakes by being clear and concise as well as allowing others to find and see clearly if there was a mistake made. 

Careful design is essential in preventing obvious errors and problems later down the line when the program reaches production and redesign could be more costly than early on in the project just started.

Requirements analysis keeps projects realistic and allows the needs to be visualized and the costs and benefits to be evaluated objectively so that the system not only meets its required needs but also is cost efficient for the business while serving its purpose.


The most significant difference among structured, object oriented, and agile methods lies in how they approach requirements and process flow. Structured methods are linear and function focused, breaking a system down into processes and data flows with the assumption that requirements are known upfront. Object oriented methods shift the focus to modeling real world entities and their interactions, emphasizing reusability and modularity. Agile abandons the idea of fixed requirements altogether, delivering working software in short iterative cycles and welcoming change throughout the project. Despite these differences, all three methods share the same ultimate goal of delivering a system that meets user needs, and all three involve some form of analysis, design, testing, and stakeholder collaboration.
