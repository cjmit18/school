

CyberStuff is a large company that sells computer hardware and software by phone and online. It processes several thousand weekly transactions on a three-shift operation and employs 50 full-time and 125 part-time employees. Lately, the billing department has experienced increased customer complaints about incorrect bills. You have been tasked with finding out why this is happening.

During your preliminary investigation, you discovered that some CyberStuff representatives did not follow established order entry procedures. You feel that with more information you might find a pattern and identify a solution for the problem, but you are still trying to figure out how to proceed.

Is a questionnaire the best approach, or would interviews be better? Whether you use interviews, a questionnaire, or both techniques, should you randomly select the participants, include an equal number of people from each shift, or use another approach? How do you proceed?

I would use both techniques including an equal number of people from all shifts but each person would be selected by random unless its been indicated that a specific person is being intentional otherwise I feel like administering a blanket questionnaire and then from that randomly selecting individuals from each shift should give someone a good idea of the workers processes and any barriers or challenges that might make them input incorrect information or at least find a pattern in how it could be happening.

Some questions I would ask would be:

1. How easy is it to enter specific information on a scale from 1 to 10
2. How responsive is the system
3. What are some bugs or features you would like to see fixed or implemented
4. Is the current system intuitive to use
5. On a scale of 1 to 10 how would you rate the current system


Interview Questions:

2. What entails your day 
3. How do our systems affect your productivity
