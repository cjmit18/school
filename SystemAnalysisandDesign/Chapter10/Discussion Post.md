Doug’s Sporting Goods sells hiking and camping supplies. The company has grown considerably in the last two years. They want to develop a customer order entry system and hired your IT consulting firm to advise them about development strategies. They lean toward in-house development because they do not want to depend on outside vendors and suppliers for technical support and upgrades. They also say they are not interested in selling online, but that could change. They want to meet with you tomorrow to make a decision. What will you say to them at the meeting?

At tomorrow's meeting with Doug's Sporting Goods, I would open by acknowledging their growth and validating their desire for control, but I would be straightforward about the real costs and risks of in-house development, particularly whether they have the technical talent on staff to build and sustain a custom system long-term. I would present three options: full custom development, a commercial off-the-shelf solution, and a hybrid approach, which I would likely recommend as the best balance of control and practicality. I would also push back on their dismissal of online sales, because designing a system today that cannot support e-commerce tomorrow is a costly mistake and one that is easy to avoid if you plan for it upfront. Before any recommendation is finalized, I would ask targeted questions about their current IT staff, budget, transaction volume, and existing systems, because the right solution depends entirely on those answers.
