Remember that **Median** is resistant to extreme values, so it is the preferred measure of central tendency when data is skewed left or right.

The three measures of dispersion that are not resistant are: **Range, Standard deviation and variance.** The interquartile range is resistant. 

**The Five-Number summary** of a set of data consists of the smallest data value, Q1, the median, Q3, and the largest data value. We use this to learn information about the extremes of a data set.

- Minimum
- Q1
- M
- Q3
- Maximum

The Five-Number summary can be used to create a graph called a **Boxplot**

**Step 1.** Determine the lower and upper fences:
$Lower Fence = Q1 - 1.5(IQR)$
$Upper Fence = Q3 + 1.5(IQR)$

**Step 2.** Draw a number line long enough to include the maximum and minimum values. Insert vertical lines at Q1, M and Q3 . Enclose these vertical lines in a box.

**Step 3.** Label the lower and upper fences with a temporary mark.

**Step 4.** Draw a line from Q1 to the smallest data value that is larger than the lower fence. Draw a line from  Q3 to the largest data value that is smaller than the upper fence. These lines are called **whiskers**.

**Step 5.** Plot any data values less than the lower fence or greater than the upper fence as outliers. Outliers are marked with an asterisk(*) . Remove the temporary marks labeling the fences.
