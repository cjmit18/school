A **frequency distribution** lists each category of data and the number of occurrences of data.
- Its a good idea to add up the frequency column to make sure it equals the number of observations.

The **Relative Frequency** is the portion ( or percentage) of the observations found within a category and is found using the formula :
- Relative Frequency = Frequency / Sum of all frequencies 
- Relative Frequency Distribution lists each category together with the relative frequency.
- Add relative frequencies so that they add up to 1 exactly or more than one if in decimal form.

A **Bar Graph** is constructed by labeling each category of data on either the horizontal or vertical axis and the frequency or relative frequency of the category of the category on another axis.
- Rectangles of equal width are drawn for each category. 
- The height represents each categories frequency of relative frequency 

A **Pareto Chart** is a bar graph in which the bars are drawn in decreasing order of frequency or relative frequency.

A **Side by Side Bar Graph** is used to compare two set of data.
- Use a horizontal bar when category names are lengthy 

A **Pie Chart** is a circle divided into sectors. 
- Each sector represents a category of data. The area of each sector is proportional to the frequency of the category.
- A **Pareto Chart** is a pie chart that lists data in descending order 5->4->3->2->1
# When should you use a Bar Graph or a Pie Chart?
1. **Pie Charts**
	- Pie charts are useful when showing the division of all possible values of a qualitative variable into its parts.
	- Not as useful when comparting two values of a qualitative variable due to angles. 
	- Emphasis is places on comparing the part to the whole.
2. **Bar Graphs**
	- Bar graphs are useful when we want to compare the different parts , not the parts to the whole.
